the writing server opened in february 2024. i joined it pretty quickly, since i was excited to write puzzles.

immediately, we created both the #puzzle-ideas and the #shitposts channels, and started saying stuff in them. it felt like less stuff than in previous years, but maybe this is because the last time we did this was mystery hunt with a lot more people writing.

for most of february, we worked on theme proposals. i mainly worked on one proposal, which, as is common, i will not say out loud just in case i want to use the theme elsewhere. i also looked into the thread for the starship saga theme proposal since it was the one i was the next most interested in. i had a small amount of familiarity with two things it was based on (zero escape and outer wilds), but it seemed like a cool plot and could easily lead to cool mechanics.

story stuff

starship saga ended up winning. i quickly wanted to get involved with the story. there were so many details to iron out that i can't remember all of them. here are some that i remember:

  • how should viewing puzzles that you can't see on your current timeline work? also, would you need to re-solve puzzles that you have solved on previous timelines?
  • what should the final problem with the spaceship be, and how would we make it clear that you need to throw out your watches to fix the problem?
  • how old should matt and emma be?
  • some questions about some pieces of information being locked behind doing other specific things
  • what happens if matt and emma die for real after yeeting the watches?

also, upon seeing some of the art, i exclaimed to my girlfriend (my only one at that time) "wow, they put bondage in my teammate hunt!?"

a couple tiny and irrelevant nitpicks i had about the art were that the vent appears to lead the wrong way vertically, and the innovation art isn't entirely accurate, but the innovation drawing is close enough to be recognizable, and the vent art is constrained by the story going in reading order (which means downwards). but the effort of the drawing more than makes up for any inaccuracies of the art.

puzzles i didn't spend 4000 hours on

the actual thing that lasted 4000 hours was a nomifactory server that i was playing on at that time, that i finished around the time teammate hunt started.

innovation

this was the very first non-test puzzle submitted to puzzup. i knew pretty quickly that i wanted to write a puzzle about innovation. however, it took a few months for me to start making plans about it.

a few months into writing, abhijit put out a call for puzzles that have a lot of sentences and/or numbers, for an alien language round. i felt that innovation would work for this, so i volunteered. i decided that an easy way to implement this was for the captain and the alien playing against each other.

the first thing i did was go through the age 7+ cards to see what cards have effects that are easily translated. i found combustion (age 7 red), rocketry (age 8 blue), composites (age 9 red), refrigeration and sanitation (age 7 yellow), and databases (age 10 green).

then i planned out the game. the game that was planned out is basically unchanged from the game that actually appeared in the hunt, except without any of the stuff that happened before turn 1, and a couple of smaller details of turn 4 being different.

i and abhijit took some time to discuss what extra words in the alien language we needed. he needed "fragment", so i decided to use it for "card". i needed a way to say "score pile", "tuck", and "hand". we decided on those being "pile" (a new word), "store", and to use the posessive for a card in hand.

to write the puzzle and the solution, i had to copy down many of the cards that were important from yucata, and place them all on a gslides presentation. the postprodding didn't want gslides, so i had to take screenshots of every slide and attach them to a gdoc. i also had to write the process of figuring out the podomorph language, which might have been harder to write about than the innovation deductions.

this is another instance of "i wrote a puzzle about a board game that is unreasonably hard", which means that i had to answer most of the hints for this puzzle. this puzzle was hard enough that the creator of innovation had to ask for a hint on this puzzle.

and that just reminded me that i still need to acquire innovation ultimate

navigation

my original idea for this round was for the puzzles to reveal a bunch of constellations that were like 3d curve datas. however, i found curve datas way too hard to write, so it was tabled for some time. at the end we made a decision to simplify the round to be a bunch of puzzles on a grid with no extractions.

we ended up on 3 types of minipuzzles for the 3 axes pretty quickly. my original thoughts were slitherlink, minesweeper, and strands, on 15x15 (large) and 8x8 (small) grids. the strands were written first because they were hardest to write to match up with the other types.

i testsolved checkmate, which took me and 2 others a couple hours. we decided that that was probably too long, so we decided as a team to shrink the puzzles from 15x15 and 8x8 to 13x13 and 7x7.

all the while, i was trying to think about how to make the minesweepers interesting. i decided that i definitely wanted to do something with minecraft, so i wrote that 7x7 first. it initially was in an inventory, but this meant that testsolvers didn't want to rotate items in crafting recipes. so i built it in an actual world on item frames, and rotated the item frames. i made sure the puzzle was still solvable ignoring the rotations of the items in the frames though.

afterwards, i came up with the colors idea. i decided that liar clues were an easy color to add, and eventually i settled on using 3x3 mines and tapa-like clues as the other 2 colors. these could translate in minecraft to using mineral blocks and having shaped recipes.

finally, came the slitherlinks. the group working on them decided that slitherlink was too hard to make interesting, so eventually it was replaced with nonograms with tetris pieces and also some akari lights.

during the hunt, we noticed a transcription error in the strands that made them not match the minesweeper. changing a cell in minecraft would have involved having to reopen the game, replace a block, take another screenshot at the same precise location, and figure out how to make the corrected picture show up in the puzzle. therefore, we ended up changing the strands, which had the side effect of making the castling part a lot more confusing since the pieces didn't need to castle to spell out their words.

stuff that happened during hunt

before hunt started, i said (to teammate, not to the public because it's potentially spoilers) that i was excited to see top teams lose teammate hunt for the first time.

podomorph stuff

since i wrote the puzzle that involves the most blobbish, i was pretty well equipped to be one of the understanders of the transmission submissions. the main thing i had to do was to understand numbers, and also see what else the teams have been telling us.

initially my decision was that if actual limbs were involved, i would accept anything with the correct coordinates, and otherwise i would require extra context. however, i later decided that this was too nice, and required the numbers to actually involve things moving for the podomorphs to understand them. the rescuer podomorphs can't read arrows, so they can't distinguish between 11 and 41 or something. (the podomorph playing innovation is a special one who can read english but isn't on that rescue mission)

if your submission got a response that is a message from the podomorphs' point of view (i.e. in first person), i was probably the one who reviewed your submission. most of the other reviewers wrote in third person for rejections and no message for acceptances.

i was considering responding in blobbish but that probably would have been more difficult to read than necessary, and i'm not sure it was possible on teammate's admin panel. i did send a translation of my message to adal into blobbish through discord dms, though i failed to remember that podomorphs only have 6 limbs, and gave this one 8.

near the end time of the hunt, i reviewed some responses from another team i personally knew. i rejected their first submission because they were not speaking blobbish at all, but decided to accept the non-moving images in their last transmission because it was less than 1 hour before the hunt end time.

hints

i answered most of the hints for the puzzles that i wrote. this made me realize that one word you could use to describe my puzzles was "unforgiving". innovation required keeping track of a lot of details of the game state over time, while masters of the craft had a lot of items that need to be placed, and a single incorrectly placed item will mark the entire grid wrong.

i also answered hints for some other puzzles that i didn't write. i think i answered mostly hints for puzzles i was very familiar with, the most hinted of which were pangrams/paingram, zoomer ruleset rulecon, the rest of the blobbish puzzles, and fauna on flora. i also answered hints for the two very most important metas (hull breach and built to spec). i was actually pretty unfamiliar with built to spec beforehand, so i had to read through the solution.
