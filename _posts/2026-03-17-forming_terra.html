so the theme was "archipelago hunt". i initially thought it referred to archipelago randomizer (which is not too different from the actual theme tbf), but that was apparently not the intention of the theme proposal.

puzzles

spirit island

as soon as i saw the 2x3 shape, i knew that i wanted to write a puzzle about the thematic boards, using braille as extraction. i decided that i wanted it to be pretty simple, which at first was to use 13 fear cards to clear the island. however, i found that fear cards were a bit too narrow in what they could do, so i switched to power cards. power cards were also good because i could use the energy costs and speeds as ordering.

i quickly wrote down some power cards i wanted to use of the speeds that i wanted. initially, i was going to make the 7-cost fast power be a slow power and have some spirit that can make it fast, because the only 7-cost fast power in the game can destroy invaders in any land with presence, so it only being able to destroy invaders on two boards was pretty restrictive. however, i felt that was inelegant, so i eventually made exaltation of the incandescent sky work. the restrictions that only two boards could have invaders in lands with presence made the rest of the puzzle easier to write.

it took a lot of effort to make the pieces fit competently. i had to make the background transparent, which i did by hand imperfectly, and then i used photopea to rotate the boards by 60 degrees so they could fit together. i also needed to ask the tech people to halve the size of the grid squares so i could have the boards actually snap together without any of them poking outside the piece.

square

this was originally going to be a puzzle about square dancing. then i heard from the headitors that pan's laboratory was mostly research puzzles, so i decided to see if i could instead write a logic puzzle.

i decided on a 10x10 grid with the extraction method being to use binary on the clues around the perimeter. i decided on castle wall and yajilin clues pretty easily since those were the two loop genres involving arrow clues that i was most familiar with.

i started by making the clues on the outside pretty restrictive. then i worked inwards with some tweaking, and somehow i managed to find a configuration that worked and was logically solvable after only like 2 tries.

the editors told me that i should have the center piece clue to look around the border, so i made the loop say "read binary cw from nw corner", and the shaded cells say "read loop". i figured that this was hard enough to unscramble that i didn't worry about teams unscrambling this. (and they needed to have the clues around the square figured out anyways)

the 00000 at the end significantly tripped up a testsolving group, who guessed an enum of 8 letters, including appending an h to the end of the correct answer once. they took until solving the entire logic puzzle to realize that the last "letter" was 00000. this did not trip up as many teams doing the puzzle during the hunt.

art

laboratory

there was much deliberation on how the island should look, and exactly what type of thing the crafting materials should come from. the wood being from a forest and the stone being from a mountain were pretty obvious.

the iron originally was going to come from some sort of mine, but we decided that we didn't want this island to have any sort of civilization. i suggested either a different mountain that was banded iron or a meteorite, and we eventually agreed on the meteorite. i think i modeled this crater drawing after a meteorite crater from ae2, but i'm not certain.

the redstone was even harder. this would have appeared after laura and sia appeared, so i proposed there being something like a technology junk pile. one of my earlier ideas was for a spaceship that looked like the arkose from teammate hunt to crash on a beach. (unfortunately arkose the mineral is not really red) some others wanted a "crimson sands" or something along those lines. i was worried about teams thinking that was red sand, but i stopped being worried because red sand isn't used in all that many crafting recipes anyways. we ended up on a mixture of both ideas, where the inator exploded and scattered all of its dust all over a beach. mark also made sure that laura and sia used a lot of red in their mechanical constructions.

the quartz was decided on being some sort of underground crystal cave pretty early on. i colored it purple because it was from the nether.

while drawing the island, i decided to take the rgb values of pixels in minecraft blocks directly, and use those colors in the drawing. i used colors from dirt, stone, oak logs, oak leaves, snow, lava, sand, lit redstone dust, unlit redstone dust, and nether portal. i don't remember what the meteorite or ocean colors were from.

i also had to draw icons for the individual biomes because we needed a way to show which biome a piece came from. i just drew a tree, a smaller mountain, the meteorite, some sand, and a purple crystal.

inator

as i said in the previous section, for laboratory meta reasons, we wanted laura and sia's machinery to have a lot of red components and circuitry. so i decided to draw a pink blobby thing, but make it look cylinder-ish. then, as the story dictated, i added some red things to make it look like it had a face, and then added some labelled dials on it, one of which is an upside-down nine. initially i had the adjacent dials be an 8 and a capital l, but someone said that made it too obvious, so i made the 7 upright.

i did not too much extra detail because my girlfriend's apple pencil ran low on battery when she was out of town, and she still had the charging piece with her.

i also drew the picture of the inator exploding. to do this, all i needed to do was to add some red/orange/yellow fireworks around the drawing of the inator.

the web

for this island, we wanted a web of islands with bridges that was abandoned. i drew 6 very dusty islands with some very rusty bridges between them and a large cobweb covering them. this first concept art had a very top-down view:

then i was told that the bridges seemed to clue something, so i changed it so that there was only at most 1 bridge between any pair of islands. i also wanted to look at the web from a better perspective that was not straight down. i mostly left the other details unchanged though.

i considered making the graph formed by the islands and bridges resemble the graph of my polycule (at that time), but decided against it. in theory, all that would have been needed was to add 2-3 more bridges.

it would have looked something like this. if i were drawing this around the time the hunt started, i would have added a bridge between the very top island and the top right island. if i were drawing my polycule graph now, i would add a bridge between the left island and the bottom left island. (the people in the two graphs are not in the same positions. also these don't include any relationships involving someone who is not in this group of 6.)

i also wasn't sure what message this would come across as conveying, if i drew the abandoned island with this graph. i did at some point consider drawing the bridges of the dirty island to be past relationships between me and 5 others and the clean island to be current relationships between a different group of me and 5 others.

i also made a "cleaned" version for use after the meta gets solved. this was not that difficult, i just had to hide a few layers (i.e. the ones with the cobweb and its shadows, the dust, and the rusty bridges) and add a few layers for the cleaned bridges and bread-like coloring on the islands.

(a)isle

the instructions for drawing (a)isle was that the meta grid was a pathway across the island, and with a bunch of animals or houses around that pathway. naturally, this would lead to a rainbow-shaped island. i also decided to draw just houses so i didn't have to draw animals.

i'm much more of a drawer of inanimate objects. however, one of my girlfriends told me that being horny is a good motivator for drawing, so i should maybe try drawing characters that i would find attractive.

i spent possibly too much effort making the perspective of the grid actually correct. but because of the grid, i decided that i might as well use the grid for other things. so all of the houses were drawn in grid-aligned spots. i hope that didn't accidentally throw anyone off. (i don't know if it was even noticeable tbh)

crater

for this, i first had to draw the ship. i took the drawing of laboratory and basically added some redstone circuitry with dust and repeaters on top of the land and some slime and honey blocks below the island. i did realize that those two things don't really work together on the same redstone build, but by that time i didn't want to change anything. then i added some brioche dust to the coasts of the island as another magical reason for the ship to be able to move.

i also added a bread flag, with colors copied from the minecraft bread texture. i probably could have added pan colors but i didn't think of it at that time.

then it was time to add the crater. first, i removed most of the trees and a lot of the grass from the forest, and added some destroyed trees to the water right off the coast. then, since the island already had a crater, i copied the lava color and drew a large lava pool where i erased the grass. then i added the meteorite with the same color and brush shape as the original meteorite. (the shape was called "evolve". it's also the same brush shape i used for the bridges in web)

then i changed the redstone circuitry to deactivate everything to the left of the crater.

atoll

the instructions i was given was "12 islands in a circle, none of which stand out too much from the others".

first of all, i needed to draw the circular shape of the atoll. that was easy enough, just draw a music disc made of sand.

next, i needed to fit 12 roughly circular islands on that circle. that was a lot harder. i needed to draw the islands smaller and closer together than i was hoping.

then, i decided to make the atoll slightly flooded, which involved adding a transparent watery layer on top of the yellow sand layer, where i drew flooding in the gaps between the islands.

finally, i needed to draw stuff on the islands. i was originally going to put different foliage on the islands, but i decided that i didn't feel like switching brushes that much, so i just put the same type of tall grass on all of them.

this was also drawn before i figured out how to rotate images in procreate, so the island looked like a zero.

this is the only round art without shadows. this matters because at some point the people coding stuff wanted the background art with transparent backgrounds. all of the other art cast shadows on the ocean which was done with the multiply blending mode, which just became the base gray color when the ocean background was removed.

i don't quite remember how i fixed the shadows, but i think something that would have been better than what i actually did was just make another layer below everything, then draw like a silhouette of the entire island including the shadows that is water colored, but extend all of the borders a bit.

i did try using one specific option in procreate (i don't remember which option it was. probably alpha lock) that made it only possible to draw on the exact silhouette of the layers above, but that didn't combine well with the shadow layer either. this was probably because the shadow layer was partially transparent, and none of the blend modes seem to work all that well with transparent layers.

thinking back on that, the blue i originally used for the oceans (which is the same color as the background color of the web draft drawing) is a lot less light than the blue used in the background of the world page. maybe i should have used a lighter color for that.

desert

there were basically no restrictions on this island, it was supposed to just look like a desert. this took me way too long to decide on a shape for.

i eventually decided to make the island pyramid-shaped, because of the egyptian pyramids and also i had "the 10000 puzzle pyramid" in my mind. (apparently it's a puzzle from mystery hunt 2015. i don't remember where i've heard the name before, since i didn't get into puzzlehunts until 2020.)

it's kinda hard to draw pyramids convincingly. this island basically just looked like a quadrilateral with a ridge in the middle.

maybe i could have done something puzzly with the cacti on the island, like making the indexing spell out "add" or something. but idk if that was possible with these cactus shapes, i just chose the first 3 cactus shapes i could think of.

someone mentioned that this art was the most puzzly-looking non-puzzly art they have ever seen. this was not on purpose. (except for maybe the grid alignment on (a)isle)

ausralia

i testsolved the meta. i think i ended up placing all of the pieces for the metas correctly, but not all of the individual pieces, and i did not end up finishing the puzzle. i feel like the puzzle could have had a lot more clues.

after getting spoiled on the puzzle after doing most of the testsolving, i offered to draw some art for it.

i didn't quite understand the cutscene before it properly, so i instead drew the final island as all of the previous rounds gathered together in a way corresponding to the final metameta. this did not quite match the dialogue written at that time, so we ended up changing the plot in response to this because it's easier to change story than art.

i initially added a stage to the atoll, but mark asked me to remove it so he could add some character stuff himself. he added a lot of other things, such as the sign on (a)isle, and all of the characters.

this is also the art that ended up as the final postcard you get from finishing the hunt.

at some point i looked over this art, and i thought that you could almost read something from it. someone else said the letters were pchno (in reading order), which could be interpreted as the 5 most important elements for life.

mark's character art

(i didn't draw any characters, mark did all of the character art)

we went through only a few drafts of laura and sia. initially, sia's tail was a lot thinner, but because her skin and tail were both pink, i thought it made her look too rat-like. so mark made her tail thicker, which fixed the issue.

when i saw the first few drawings of pan, i thought his face's color texture was too "flat". (i.e. there was not enough color variation on his face) this was definitely made better by the rendering, since at least there were shadows giving a bit of variation on his face and making it darker.

gaia was always intended to be a tree character. opinions on this being the third gph in a row with a tree character were quite divided, but we did get enough of a majority to go ahead and make gaia a tree. probably to appease the others, at some point we decided that gaia was gonna just be big branch from last year. i'm not sure i agreed with this but i didn't really feel like suggesting anything different. though maybe this would have been a good character to draw as a cute girl akin to stelle from gph 2024.
